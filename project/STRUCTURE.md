# VAACA as a non-profit, with services delivered through IAFN

The business-model specification proposed two new entities: VAACA Association
and a VAACA Services subsidiary. This replaces that with something simpler and
closer to what already exists — **VAACA stays a non-profit and nothing else, and
the services are delivered by IAFN, a founding member institution.**

That is not a workaround. It is what the institutional architecture already
said: *IAFN equips the ecosystem; VAACA connects the ecosystem.* The subsidiary
was a way of giving VAACA a commercial arm it does not need, when the member
whose mandate is education, certification and capacity is already at the table.

---

## 1. What each entity does

**VAACA — non-profit.** Coordination, representation, policy dialogue,
convening, and the publication of its own work. Concretely: the General
Assembly, the Coordination Council, the ten sector councils, the chambers, the
chapters, the Regulatory Observatory, the Knowledge Commons, the forums, and
the standards the Readiness Framework rests on.

**IAFN — founding member, service provider.** Professional education,
certification administration, institutional advisory, and commissioned
research. IAFN contracts with the client, carries the delivery risk, and pays
VAACA a licence on what it delivers under VAACA's brand, curriculum and
credential.

### Which engine sits where

| Engine | Entity | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Membership | VAACA | The association's own dues. Cannot sit anywhere else. |
| Strategic partnerships | VAACA | Institutional, funds ecosystem development, not a service sale. |
| Events | VAACA | The forums are the association convening its own members. |
| Research — reports, enterprise licences | VAACA | Publishing and licensing work it produced itself. |
| Intelligence subscriptions | VAACA | The Observatory is VAACA's own output; a subscription licenses it. |
| Data products | VAACA | Same — the directory and the graph are the association's record. |
| Platform services | VAACA | It owns the platform. |
| Professional education | **IAFN** | Its existing mandate. |
| Certification | **IAFN** | It administers; the credential is VAACA's and stays VAACA's. |
| Advisory | **IAFN** | Commercial consulting, delivered to a client, at risk. |
| Commissioned research | **IAFN** | A client pays for a specific answer. VAACA's own research does not work that way. |

The line is not "commercial versus non-commercial" — a subscription is
commercial. The line is **whether VAACA is selling its own institutional output
or selling someone a service.** A non-profit may do the first. The second is
what IAFN is for.

## 2. What it does to the numbers

The model now splits every line by entity, on the **Entity Split** sheet.

| XAF | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | Y4 | Y5 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| VAACA own revenue | 159m | 342m | 686m | 1,155m | 1,740m |
| IAFN services | 90m | 190m | 373m | 663m | 902m |
| Ecosystem | 249m | 532m | 1,059m | 1,818m | 2,642m |
| Licence to VAACA (12%) | 11m | 23m | 45m | 80m | 108m |
| **VAACA total income** | **170m** | **365m** | **731m** | **1,235m** | **1,848m** |
| VAACA operating surplus | (56m) | (35m) | 78m | 280m | 588m |

IAFN delivers about a third of ecosystem revenue throughout.

### The cost of the structure, stated plainly

**Break-even moves from Year 2 to Year 3, and the funding requirement roughly
triples** — from a 31m deficit in Year 1 to a cumulative 92m by the end of
Year 2.

The reason is not subtle. VAACA gives up education, certification and advisory
— margin-rich lines — and keeps almost the whole fixed cost base: the
secretariat, the platform, the chapters, marketing, premises. The 12% licence
is far smaller than the gross surplus those engines were contributing.

**The licence rate that would restore Year 2 break-even is 30.6%.** That is the
number the arrangement has to answer for. 12% is a placeholder, not a
negotiated figure, and the honest options are:

1. **Set the licence nearer 25–30%**, which is defensible if VAACA supplies the
   brand, the curriculum, the credential and the member channel — IAFN's cost
   of customer acquisition through VAACA is close to zero.
2. **Accept Year 3 break-even and fund the gap**, which is a legitimate choice
   for a founding institution and simply means raising more.
3. **Move one engine back.** Certification is the candidate: the credential is
   VAACA's, the standard is VAACA's, and only administration is a service.

I would not recommend the third without care — it is how an exception becomes a
commercial arm by increments, which is the thing this structure exists to
avoid.

## 3. The related-party problem

**This is the part that matters more than the arithmetic.**

IAFN is a founding member of VAACA. It sits in the General Assembly. The
institutional architecture names an IAFN seat on the Governing Council. And
under this structure it is also VAACA's sole service provider, earning from the
ecosystem VAACA convenes.

That is a related-party arrangement between a non-profit and a member of its own
governing body. The concentration test passes easily — the licence is 5.9–6.4%
of VAACA's income, well inside the 15% ceiling — but concentration is the easy
test and it is not the one that matters here.

What matters is that the arrangement is not decided by the people who benefit
from it. Four rules, and they should be in the Charter rather than in a policy
note:

1. **Recusal.** The IAFN representative does not participate in, and is not
   counted for quorum on, any decision concerning the service agreement, the
   licence rate, its renewal, or the scope of what IAFN delivers.
2. **Publication.** The agreement, the licence rate and the annual amount paid
   are published in the Transparency Centre. A related-party arrangement that
   is public is governable; one that is not is a finding waiting to happen.
3. **Non-exclusivity.** VAACA does not grant IAFN exclusivity over education or
   certification delivery. The institution's own stated purpose is to
   *strengthen existing institutions rather than replace them*; an exclusive
   channel would do the opposite to every other training provider in the
   region, and several are prospective members.
4. **Term and review.** The agreement runs for a fixed term — three years is
   the natural first one — and is renewed by a decision of the Council with
   IAFN recused, against published delivery and quality measures.

The Ethics, Standards & Professional Conduct Committee is the body that should
own rules 1 and 2, and the architecture already calls for it to be structurally
protected from commercial influence. **It needs to exist before the agreement is
signed, not after** — a committee constituted to review an arrangement already
in place reviews nothing.

## 4. What this does not resolve

**The legal form.** Whether VAACA can hold membership dues, licence income and
subscription revenue while retaining non-profit status under Cameroonian and
OHADA law is a question for counsel, not for this document. So is whether the
licence is taxable to VAACA, and whether IAFN's use of the VAACA credential
creates a permanent establishment anywhere. The architecture is right that the
structure should not be assumed before that analysis.

What this structure does do is make the analysis *simpler*: one non-profit and a
contract, rather than a non-profit, a subsidiary, and the consolidation and
transfer-pricing questions that come with it.

**IAFN's own viability.** Nothing here models IAFN. It takes about a third of
ecosystem revenue and carries the delivery cost of education, certification and
advisory — but whether that is a business is IAFN's question to answer, and
VAACA should see the answer before signing. A service provider that cannot fund
its own delivery is a dependency, not a partner.

**Who owns what.** The credential, the curriculum, the research methodology and
the Readiness Framework are VAACA's and should be stated to be VAACA's in the
agreement. Materials IAFN develops to teach them are a harder case and should
be settled in writing before the first programme, not after it succeeds.

---

## 5. What changes in the platform

Little, and that is the point — the platform models the institution, not the
revenue.

- Nothing in the code represents a commercial entity, so nothing had to be
  unwound.
- IAFN already appears as a founding coalition member on `/institution`,
  described as *technical secretariat and evidence base*. That description is
  now incomplete: it is also the service provider, and the Transparency Centre
  will need to say so.
- When the Transparency Centre is built, the IAFN agreement, the licence rate
  and the amount paid are among the first things it should publish.
